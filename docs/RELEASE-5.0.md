# 5.0 · Spacetime technical art

The eight abilities now use distinct visual structures, authored layers and timing: a segmented Fresnel shield, scanned ship hologram, textured accretion disk with an occluding core and screen-space lensing, an ionized lance with a coherent core and animated helical strands, intersecting sophon orbits, folded dimensional planes, a curvature wake and staged broadcast wavefronts. A new original four-quadrant emission atlas supplies shockwave, plasma, mist and flare detail. Skill light sources illuminate nearby models. Enemy impacts use textured debris and pressure waves.

Environment geometry has near, middle and distant layers with different scrolling speeds. Distance-dependent material tint, depth fog and translucent scattering layers separate the combat plane from orbital scenery. Each chapter retains its own Meshy structures, distant environment and palette. Camera projection and distant environment framing adapt to the actual browser aspect ratio, preventing stretched spacecraft in narrow windows. Normal weapon tracers are shorter so automatic fire no longer appears as a continuous beam.

Upgrade cards have explicit high-contrast text colors in normal, hover and keyboard focus states; readable 15–16 px descriptions; a stronger opaque backdrop; and a single-column layout on narrow screens. The compact combat HUD remains.

Seventeen sample-based sound effects replace the earlier simple oscillator sounds. Original electroacoustic stems were generated through Merouter / Eleven Music, then edited into distinct attacks, bodies, stereo reflections and tails. Shots and impacts have three variants. Skill voices are reserved within the ten-voice budget, music ducks during skills, effect sources pause with the upgrade screen, and the mix reserves peak headroom. The 4.0 opening and combat scores remain.

Enemy pressure now includes elite fire-control ships and two-sided escorts. Sniper attacks track the pilot during a visible charge, freeze the lock, then release a three-dart burst. Decoys redirect tracking, gravity bends momentum through softened forces and damping, and shields provide a measurable defensive window. Standard enemy durability rises modestly with mission time, while encounter population and live projectiles are bounded. The 120-second minimum remains. The stretched Guzheng warning oval has been replaced by open rails and moving edge chevrons.

Two original miniature concepts were generated, reconstructed through Meshy, imported into Blender and exported as ~2K-triangle game assets with 1K PBR maps. The supply capsule's missing core was restored with separate beveled emissive geometry. Enemy ammunition and pickups now use those models. Projectile integration uses bounded substeps; pickup attraction uses damped velocity instead of position snapping. Impact plumes inherit hit direction, rotate and decelerate without Earth gravity.

Thirteen original Mandarin male narration clips were generated through Merouter / Gemini TTS using Charon (no real actor imitation). A dedicated speech channel prioritizes tactical information, avoids overlap, expires stale lines, pauses appropriately and ducks music/effects. Subtitles follow the spoken clip.

The new Wallfacer Archive stores the last 30 sorties in this browser, including outcomes, chapter score, kills, recoveries, upgrade choices and successful skill activations. It supports JSON export and honest unfinished/abandoned states. It is local browser storage, not cloud sync or video replay. React Bits AnimatedContent, SpotlightCard and StarBorder join DecryptedText, ClickSpark and CountUp, with motion assigned to chapter entry, upgrade decisions, readiness, debrief and archive. Reduced-motion preferences are respected.

Validation: 79 Unity Play Mode gameplay/load checks passed, including narration, telegraph/decoy interaction, real journal events, open corridor rails and 30/60 Hz physics consistency. Blender source reopen, editable parameter probes and GLB reimport passed. A 72-second stationary auto-fire test at Standard across three seeded pairs recorded 27/25/26 hits without skills and 7/12/13 with a defensive skill policy (no upgrades); this establishes skill utility, not human difficulty. Archive persistence/corrupt-storage/retention tests passed. Actual Play Mode images cover three chapters and eight skills. Final browser/performance and deployment receipts follow.

Combat readability follow-up: real damage amounts are projected from Unity into the web HUD, with short aggregation windows and bounded lifetimes. Damaged enemies reveal their remaining armor briefly; the pilot has a following armor bar and explicit stable/damaged/critical/protected/reconstructing states. Hit shells retain the imported PBR surface. Hostile darts have stronger cores and trails, sniper locks are wider, and supply capsules have labels, pulsing emission and at most three local lights. The app now fills the dynamic viewport without a minimum-height crop, with narrow and short-window layouts.

Archive browser verification retained the completed 132-second sortie after a new page load and produced valid JSON with its 54 events. Blob file saving is not supported by the automation browser; the UI therefore exposes both a normal download link and selectable JSON as a fallback.

Final feedback runtime: browser combat showed actual damage values, enemy armor, typed supplies and the pilot critical state. 960×540 and 390×720 layouts filled the viewport without document overflow. Three eight-second samples measured 97.2, 96.3 and 114.9 FPS on this machine (worst frame 53, 19 and 60 ms); these are local measurements, not cross-device guarantees. No browser errors/warnings were observed.
